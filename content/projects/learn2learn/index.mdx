## The idea

Starting secondary school asks a lot of a child: more teachers, more subjects,
more homework, more expected of them. The skill underneath all of it — how to
actually learn something — is almost never taught directly.

Learn2Learn came out of a conversation before my daughter's first year. The
deal was that all I wanted from that year was for her to learn how to learn.
Not top marks. Just get hold of that one idea. It took the pressure off, and it
worked. My son starts now, so it is written down.

## The method

Four phases that fit around a normal school week:

- **Introduction** — in class. You are meeting the topic, not understanding it
  yet.
- **Learn** — at home, in your own time. Read it, make sense of it, write down
  the questions you still have. This is the phase that matters and the one
  allowed to take longest.
- **Revise** — come back to what you worked out, rather than reading it cold
  again.
- **Revise the revision** — flash cards, ten minutes across subjects, building
  a store of things you can reach for.

Underneath all four: *the more familiar it gets, the smaller it gets.* A topic
that filled an evening in September is a five-minute review by Christmas. Not
because the child worked harder — because it shrank.

Phase two loops back to phase one. Working through something at home often
sends you back to the classroom with a question, and asking it is the hardest
part for a child three weeks into a new school. It is also the part that lasts
longest.

## The build

The first version was generated by an AI site builder from the idea alone, and
it invented its own framework — four abstract pillars nobody had decided on,
written confidently enough to pass for the real thing. Replacing that with the
actual method was the entire job; the design and hosting were fine.

It now runs on Cloudflare Workers under 1zero9.com, with every trace of the
original platform removed — its hosting config, an auth module that only worked
behind that platform's headers, and a build plugin that existed to serve it.

The site is content-led, so the tests assert the content: the four phases in
order, the line the method rests on, the statement that this is unaffiliated
with any school or exam board, and a guard that the invented pillars cannot
creep back.

## Why it matters

There is no qualification behind this and no institution attached, and the site
says so plainly. That honesty is the point: it is one parent's observations,
written down in case they are useful to somebody else's child.
